## Facts a new session needs

- S3 serial port when on its **UART** USB socket: `/dev/cu.usbmodem5C843177861`
  (a CH343 bridge). The other socket (native USB) flashes but shows no log.
  The WROOM's port is not yet known — check `ls /dev/cu.*` after plugging in.
- To read serial without resetting or holding the port, the previous
  session used pyserial from PlatformIO's Python
  (`$(head -1 $(which pio) | sed 's/^#!//')`) with `dtr=False, rts=False`
  set before `open()`. Close the user's `pio device monitor` first or the
  port is busy.
- PlatformIO and OpenSCAD (snapshot cask) are installed via Homebrew.
  Homebrew's `python3` shadows the system one; the simulator needs
  `/usr/bin/python3` (it has numpy).
- Inside unit partitions: `min_spiffs.csv` (two 1.9 MB slots, for OTA). The
  next USB flash of the WROOM installs this layout.
- LED colours are gamma-corrected (`gamma8`) and capped at 40 %.
- Images from the owner often arrive as `.heic` in `~/Downloads`; convert
  with `sips -s format jpeg`. Video: extract frames with `ffmpeg`.

## Build day, in order

### 0. Faceplate (printing now)
When done: snap one button into a hole, slide one LED strip piece into a
channel, put the speaker in the ring, set the plate on the shell. All four
should fit without force.

### 1. Link test — the gate (~30 min)
Nothing gets soldered until this passes.

1. Plug **both** boards into the laptop. S3 on its UART socket.
2. Flash:
   ```sh
   pio run -e linktest_outside -t upload --upload-port /dev/cu.usbmodem5C843177861
   pio run -e linktest_inside  -t upload --upload-port <WROOM port>
   ```
3. Desk check, 30 s: open the inside monitor
   (`pio device monitor -p <WROOM port>`). Expect `outside unit found`,
   then a line every 10 s with near-zero loss. The S3's onboard LED is green.
4. Real test: WROOM stays on the laptop near the chime box. S3 goes
   outside where the button will be, on a phone power bank. **Close the
   door.** LED must be green.
5. Wait 10 minutes. The laptop prints `LINK TEST RESULT` and `OVERALL: PASS`
   or `FAIL`.
   - Pass criteria: unicast delivery ≥ 99.9 %, no silence ≥ 1 s,
     average RSSI ≥ −80 dBm.
6. **If FAIL:** stop and discuss. Options in order: move the inside board
   closer to the door, external-antenna board, or the Cat6 pull from
   CLAUDE.md (check for conduit).
7. Open question: the test uses WiFi channel 1. Once the inside unit joins
   home WiFi, ESP-NOW must run on the **router's** channel. Ask the owner
   for it; if known, rerun with `-DLINK_CHANNEL=<n>` added to both
   linktest envs.

### 2. Two-board firmware (Claude writes it; owner solders meanwhile)
Must-have for the day, in this order:
1. Outside: send each pad press to the inside unit over ESP-NOW (unicast),
   packet = pad index + grid position. Broadcast grid phase every bar.
2. Inside: same engine and samples, plays the events quantised to the same
   grid, locks to the outside's phase. A lost packet must not break sync.
3. Inside: volume pot on GPIO34 (ADC1).
4. Long-press centre pad (pad 5) = plain 2-second chime, both units.
5. WiFi + OTA on both units. Credentials in `include/secrets.h`
   (gitignored; template `include/secrets.example.h`).

Nice-to-have, if time allows: quiet hours (NTP, cut outside volume after
22:00), phone notification (MQTT or Home Assistant webhook), performance
replay.

Keep every new piece testable on the host like `lib/engine/`, and add
native tests.

### 3. Solder and assemble — outside unit
Do these **in this order**:

1. **LED strips first, on the bench.** Cut three 3-LED pieces at the copper
   pads. Solder them as one chain: row 1 out (right end) → row 2 in (left
   end) → row 3. Arrows left to right. Flash `outside_pixeltest`
   (`pio run -e outside_pixeltest -t upload`) and watch red/green/blue walk
   through all 9. Only then slide them into the channels.
2. **Set the buck converter to 5.0 V before connecting anything to it.**
   Feed it 12 V, turn its small screw while measuring the output with a
   meter. A wrong voltage here destroys the boards.
3. **Protection board** (perfboard), between the wall wires and the buck:
   wall + → fuse (500 mA — check the cap, the bag said 0313001, which may
   be 1 A) → SS34 (stripe towards the buck) → buck IN+.
   Across IN+/GND: SMAJ15A (stripe to +) and 470 µF (stripe to GND).
   Buck OUT+ → second SS34 (stripe away from buck) → **5 V rail** (~4.6 V).
4. 5 V rail → S3 5V pin, amp VIN, LED strip 5V. 1000 µF across 5V/GND at
   the first LED (stripe to GND).
5. Buttons: every COM to GND; NO to GPIO 4, 5, 6, 7, 15, 16, 17, 18, 8
   (pads 1–9, reading order).
6. Amp: BCLK→12, LRC→11, DIN→10, GAIN→VIN, SD not connected. Speaker on
   OUT+/OUT−, never to GND.
7. LED data: GPIO13 → 330 Ω → row 1 DIN.
8. Heat-set inserts into the 4 shell corners (soldering iron, slow and
   straight). Boards on the shell floor with foam tape. Plate on with
   4× M3×8 countersunk screws.

Inside unit wiring (in the existing chime box): amp BCLK→26, LRC→25,
DIN→22, GAIN not connected; pot ends to 3V3/GND, wiper → GPIO34. Power:
IRM-10-12 → 12 V → (a) the pair to the door, (b) buck at 5.0 V → SS34 →
WROOM 5V and amp VIN.

### 4. Install — **breaker off**
1. Breaker off. Confirm 0 V with the meter at the chime box before
   touching anything.
2. Remove the old chime and 8 V transformer. Mount the IRM-10-12 in its
   place. This is the 230 V step: if in any doubt, an electrician does it.
3. Mark which of the two brown wires is +12 V.
4. Breaker on. Measure 12 V DC on the pair **at the door, before**
   connecting the outside unit. Check polarity.
5. Connect the outside unit; screw the shell to the Nisko wall box
   (horizontal slots take 52–68 mm screw spacing).

### 5. Tune and publish
- Volume (inside pot; outside amp is already at its quiet gain), LED
  brightness, quantise on/off by ear.
- Before announcing the repo: add `LICENSE` (proposed: MIT for code,
  CC BY 4.0 for `cad/` and `data/` — confirm with the owner), a
  `README.md` for humans (what, photos/video, parts, wiring, build, flash,
  print), and a 230 V safety note. CLAUDE.md is for Claude, not readers.
